# Shared decisions and tasks for community groups

Poa gives clubs and community groups a shared place for tasks, membership responsibilities, and member voting. Use it when the people who keep a group going need a clear way to decide together and pass responsibility to others.

The best part of a club is what its members bring to it: an unexpected film, a skill someone wants to teach, a project nobody would have attempted alone.

Poa gives members a way to take responsibility for the group itself: prepare events, govern shared funds, and welcome the people who will run it after them. The work and decisions stay with the organization as responsibilities change.

## Build a club that outlasts graduation

Imagine a campus makers' club where people explore ideas and build projects beyond their coursework. The club has a workshop budget, a few unfinished projects, and two coordinators graduating in May.

It could give eligible members equal weight in [binding decisions](/docs/hybridVoting). Coordinator roles handle projects and invitations; reviewers approve submitted work. Those responsibilities let a new organizer help run a workshop before taking over the wider program.

Members can propose how to use the workshop budget and check the resulting payment. As the handover approaches, the group updates roles through its authorized membership or governance process. The incoming team can see the outstanding tasks, previous spending decisions, and who is responsible for each part.

Before the coordinators leave, have their successors accept the relevant roles and run one workshop: create its tasks, review the results, and prepare its spending proposal. Check each responsibility works, then update the outgoing members' authority. [Roles and permissions](/docs/roles-and-permissions) explains what to check.

The choice of equal voting fits a club where membership is the basis of participation. It does not need contribution-weighted votes or revenue distributions to benefit from shared authority and records.

## Make next Saturday happen

A neighborhood repair group has a different challenge: enough people want to help, but a few experienced volunteers handle every review and spending question. Preparing an afternoon of repairs is a chance to share those responsibilities.

The [task board](/docs/task-manager) gives preparation work a clear outcome and reviewer. Someone new can test donated tools or prepare a station. The group could use open membership for general participation and [vouches](/docs/vouching-and-trust) from experienced members for a reviewer role. Once eligible and accepted, another volunteer can review work under that role's permissions.

Members could use equal-weight binding proposals to approve parts or room expenses. If the group wants to recognize ongoing maintenance with a stake, it can also configure [contribution rewards](/docs/contribution-and-ownership). Keep those rewards separate from the choice of who receives help.

For food collection or mutual aid, use the public board to coordinate work and keep recipients' details private. Decide how to allocate help separately from contribution rewards.

## Give someone a way to take part

Keep your existing event calendar, ticketing, and private contact records where they work best. Poa helps members coordinate preparation tasks and shared decisions; its organization records are public. Membership in Poa does not by itself grant ownership of a community building or other legal property.

When you share the organization link, invite people to a specific activity: a screening to help choose, a repair station to prepare, or a discussion to host.

Explain whether membership is open or needs approval or vouches. Name someone who can answer questions and review completed work, so joining leads naturally into taking part.
