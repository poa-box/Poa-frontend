Poa stands for **Perpetual Organization Architect**. It is open-source software for worker-owned businesses, community organizations, and collectives. It brings membership, tasks, contribution rewards, voting, and a shared treasury together under rules the group chooses. The web app lets you create and run an organization without writing code.

People can earn Shares by helping make the organization valuable. Useful work can become a recorded stake, influence over decisions, and a share when the group distributes revenue.

The group chooses how those pieces fit together. Votes can count equally among eligible members, by contribution, or through a blend. You also choose whether to award ownership for work and distribute shared funds. The purpose, membership, and rules are yours to define.

This is also a way to create a **DAO**, short for decentralized autonomous organization: a group whose shared rules and records use public blockchain infrastructure. You do not need to know that term to use Poa. Start with what your group wants to do; the [creation guide](/docs/create/) explains the setup, account requirements, and costs to consider.

## Your contribution can keep counting

A designer delivers an interface. A club member prepares a workshop. A contributor fixes a difficult bug. Each has added something the group needed.

In Poa, a task can offer an ownership reward for that work. Someone takes it on, submits the result, and receives the configured reward when a reviewer approves completion. The contribution becomes part of their recorded stake in the organization.

That stake can count toward future decisions and revenue distributions under the group's rules. It is a way for the people doing the work to build a place in what they are creating together.

![Decentral Park task showing a plant installation, a submitted result, and a reward of 50 shares](/images/product/task-detail.webp "A task in Decentral Park: the work, the submitted result, and its 50-share contribution reward in one place.")

Ownership here means participation rights inside the Poa organization. The units are nontransferable, and do not by themselves grant legal equity or copyright. [Understand what a stake gives you](/docs/contribution-and-ownership).

## Share in the value you create

A working collective can use revenue to fund its next project and choose an amount to distribute to contributors. Poa can allocate that funded amount using a recorded snapshot of participation balances, with each eligible recipient claiming their share.

This lets a group connect the rewards of a successful project with the people who helped build it. Members can inspect the allocation and its claims in the shared record.

A distribution is a deliberate, funded decision. It is separate from a task's optional cash bounty; earning a stake does not guarantee income. [Explore shared funds and revenue distributions](/docs/treasury-management).

## Give members a say that can change things

Your group can give eligible members equal votes, weight votes by contribution, or combine the two. Equal weight makes membership the basis of a decision. Contribution weight lets recorded work influence it. A blend gives both a place.

A binding proposal can authorize spending, change permissions, or update supported rules. Once it passes, is finalized, and executes successfully, the agreed action takes effect. A separate poll can help the group explore a question before committing to action.

The result is a defined way for members to shape where the organization goes and how its resources are used. [Choose how decisions work](/docs/hybridVoting).

## Share responsibility as well as votes

Members do not need a group vote for every ordinary action. Roles can give someone authority to welcome people, organize a project, or review finished work.

Clear permissions let people act within their responsibilities. A club can hand coordination to next year's members. A collective can give an experienced contributor review authority. Each can keep the work moving as the team changes. [Learn about roles and permissions](/docs/roles-and-permissions).

## Keep more ways to continue

The organization's core records and rules live on public infrastructure. Poa is open source, and a maintained, compatible interface can work with the same organization.

That reduces reliance on any single website and supports censorship resistance. It gives members ways to inspect what happened and choose the tools they use to participate. Hosting, connectivity, and maintaining alternatives still matter. [See why decentralization is useful](/docs/why-decentralization).

## Choose the organization you want to be part of

These capabilities work together, but your group chooses which to use. A club can share decisions without distributing money. A studio can connect paid work with earned ownership. An open-source collective can give maintainers a way to steer a funded roadmap.
