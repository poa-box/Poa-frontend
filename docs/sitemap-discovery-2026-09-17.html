Why Google can crawl Poa successfully while its sitemap read date stays old

Successful access does not guarantee that Google will use a sitemap to discover more pages. Direct Google guidance and closely matching firsthand reports support a separate demand and scheduling decision. That is the leading working explanation here, not a confirmed account-specific diagnosis.

Repeated manual submissions are not the proposed maintenance process. Poa already generates and publishes its sitemap automatically. The investigation found no present defect requiring a new sitemap implementation.

What we know about Poa

  • The submitted URL is https://poa.box/sitemap.xml, with Success, 50 discovered URLs, and a last-read date of May 28.
  • The user's June 18–September 14 crawl report shows 1,245 requests across both hosts, no host problems, 95% HTTP 200 responses, 99% refresh, and less than 1% discovery. These are requests, including resources and repeats, not unique or indexed pages.
  • The cooperative guide was unknown to Google in the stored inspection result. The user subsequently confirmed that Google's live test succeeded and submitted an indexing request.
  • The current sitemap contains 34 URLs and matches the committed HEAD and origin/main file exactly. All 34 live targets returned HTTP 200, correct self-canonicals, and no noindex directive in the named-review checks.
  • The sitemap is advertised in robots.txt, generated on each build, and deployed with the static site. Current cache policy requires revalidation. Matching/stale ETag and old modification-date probes returned the current file, with no false 304 observed.

These findings weaken a broad access-block explanation. They do not prove that Google fetched this exact sitemap recently. The May 28 date describes that file, not the site's last crawl. Google's Crawl Stats definitions.

What Google's documentation supports

Sitemaps have a recrawl schedule independent of ordinary page crawling. Google explicitly lists low sitemap crawl demand as a possible reason for nonprocessing. A working site can therefore receive page and resource crawls without frequent sitemap revisits. Official Sitemaps report guidance.

Google's Gary Illyes has linked sitemap handling to the pages supplied in the feed. Crawl and indexing decisions involve content quality and relative popularity; a transport-level success is only one prerequisite. This supports a demand hypothesis, not a finding that Poa is penalized or that its guides are poor. March 2023 office hours; June 2023 office hours.

Google may skip reprocessing an unchanged sitemap. That is not an explanation for every frozen Last read date: the report defines Last read as fetching, and a sitemap must be fetched before new contents can be observed. The distinction matters when interpreting successful HTTP requests versus report updates. Google's processing explanation.

Assessment and a sustainable response

  1. Leading working explanation: low priority for additional discovery under Google's independent sitemap schedule. It fits Google's direct explanation and Poa's limited first-time fetches, but aggregate percentages do not prove the cause.
  2. Remaining alternatives: old cache behavior or a sitemap processing/reporting discrepancy. Current tests do not reproduce a cache defect. No matching current Google outage was established. The unknown guide makes the issue more than a cosmetic timestamp concern.
  3. Keep the stable, automatic publication pipeline. Preserve accurate dates, canonical-only inventory, rendered articles and ordinary links from the homepage/docs hub. The main technical repairs described above already exist; avoid another rewrite without evidence.
  4. Measure useful discovery. Track whether newly published public guides are crawled, indexed and receive relevant impressions. Improving specific workflows, original examples and real references to Poa is a more defensible direction than manipulating sitemap dates. It still offers no promised Google schedule.
  5. The most direct remaining diagnostic is exact-route request evidence. Compare verified Googlebot requests for /sitemap.xml with Last read, distinguishing normal Googlebot from user-triggered inspection. No requests supports scheduling; successful delivery of the current XML with a frozen report supports a processing/reporting discrepancy; errors or stale bytes identify a technical repair. Account logs were not available in this investigation.

Daily resubmission, repeatedly renaming the file, increasing priority/changefreq, and setting every date to today are not established fixes. Google ignores priority/changefreq, and its old ping endpoint is retired. Current sitemap guidance; Ping retirement.
